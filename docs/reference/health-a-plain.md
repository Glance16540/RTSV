## How each was read

- **Seasons until a campaign is decided.** Decided means the campaign ended: one people rules beyond challenge, or the player is beaten. A campaign decided sooner is let go on turning to twenty years for the twenty-year figures, with nobody leaving it.
- **Campaigns ending in ruin.** A world where no people gained ground in twenty years while the bands held much of the map: no winner (src/sim/ruin.ts). They are counted among the decided.
- **Seasons until a rival reaches its next age.** Counted from the start, or from its last age.
- **A town left alone, all its people (grown and children) after 20 years.** Nobody visits any town in these runs, so every town is left alone. Grown people and children together, against the same at the start; the grown alone are given beside it, since a child takes fifteen years to be counted grown. Whether it is held by its houses and food is not judged.
- **A town visited every season vs left alone, after 20 years.** A visit is ten minutes of a real battle; run with VISITS=n to measure it on n campaigns (about two minutes each).
- **Towns that die out (no people at all) per 100 town-lifetimes.** A town-lifetime is taken as twenty years of a held town, and only held towns are counted. Together means each of the three within the two years before.
- **Towns left all one sex.** Of towns with anyone grown in them. A nation's men away with its armies are counted as their town's (armytown.ts).
- **Of those who reach 60, the share dead by 70.** Counted by cohort: everyone who turned sixty in a season early enough to have turned seventy before the run ended, against everyone who turned seventy from ten years in. People are not followed one by one, so those who move town, are carried off or come home older (threats.ts adds the years held) blur it a little.
- **Share of seasons an unfarmed town with no convoy is hungry, where its governor can still raise a farm.** No convoy means none was on the road to it as the season began. Can still raise a farm is the governor's own test (governor.ts), read at the end of the season: a free place among its buildings, and its stores, less the farm's price, at or above his floor.
- **Share of seasons an unfarmed town with no convoy is hungry, where no farm can be raised.** The rest of the unfarmed towns with no convoy: no free place, or not the stores.
- **Deaths from hunger in a nation, per 20 years.** Read from the log, over the first twenty years, across all three peoples of each campaign (living or not). Whether a town that lost most was besieged or cut off is not judged.
- **A nation's supplies after 20 years of peace.** The map has no upkeep but what its towns eat, so this is the food in a nation's towns over what they eat in a season. A season of peace is one at least 10 years in, after a stretch of 10 years in which the nation won or lost land in no more than 8 seasons (eight of the ten years without war). Each nation's seasons of peace are taken together as their median, and the figure is the median of the nations, so that one long-lived nation does not outweigh the rest.
- **Widest gap between two towns of one nation.** Most of the time is taken as over half of the seasons of a nation with two towns or more.
- **A nation that builds churches and keeps its towns fed, over 20 years.** A nation with a church standing for at least ten of the twenty years and hungry in under 5% of its town-seasons.
- **Holy towns with a Monastery or Convent by 20 years.** A holy town is one whose own standing is Virtuous at the end of the twentieth year, whoever holds it. The house counts when it stands finished on the town's record.
- **The player's gold at 20 years, and grain bought abroad by decree.** Gold in all the player's towns at the end of the twentieth year. Grain bought abroad is the food decree (grain.ts), read from the player's log.
- **Lords angry at 20 years, the player's and the rivals' each.** Lords of every living nation at the end of the twentieth year (nobles.ts) whose mood is -40 or under. The player played by the computer gives decrees but deals with no noble; a rival weighs its own (nobleweigh.ts).
- **Lords content at 20 years, the player's and the rivals' each.** Lords whose mood is 30 or over at the end of the twentieth year: enough to help the nation in need (nobleacts.ts).
- **A noble's plot against the player.** Every plot begun against the player (courtstory.ts), over the years the campaign ran.
- **Rival nations with a Hall of Judgement by 20 years.** Rivals alive at the end of the twentieth year; a Hall counts when it stands finished in any town of theirs.
- **With sensible decrees vs none.** Standing lost to drift alone is what drift took from a town season by season (drift.ts: its strain, its women at the tavern, the women ordered into it), before any rise and not counting war, raids or the nation's dips, summed over twenty years for every town held by one people throughout. With decrees, the player gives the sensible ones (decrees.ts) and rivals rule as they do; without, nobody gives any. The second reading sums drift's toll season by season over every town held that season by the people it began with, whether or not it is still theirs at twenty years, as a town over twenty years (proposed: the row's first reading averages only the towns still held at twenty years, and moves with which towns those are).
- **Raids on a given town.** Every strike by a band on a held town, the marauders' included, per season it was held, over the whole run.
- **A story met face to face within the first ten years.** A story waiting on the player's word face to face (encounter.ts): a thief caught, a suitor, a rival's envoy, a plotter found out. Read from the first season one waits.
- **A story begins for the player's nation.** Every story inside the story begun for the player (a thief, a killer, a rival king, a suitor, a plot), over the years the campaign ran. The computer-played player answers none of them (STORIES=answer makes it answer).
- **A thread of tales runs to its end.** A thread is a tale that an earlier answer led to and the tales after it (threadtales.ts), ended when an answer leads nowhere further or dropped when its rival or tribe is gone. The computer-played player answers no tales, so no thread is begun (STORIES=answer makes it answer).
- **A thread begun by the world.** The share of campaigns in which each kind of thing befell the player's people and its thread was begun at a telling (threadstarts.ts, worldtales.ts): a land lost to a rival, a leader's fall, a plague, a royal marriage, a hungry winter, a claimant's rising. Each begins once in a campaign at most.
- **Seasons a thief or a killer is at large in a town, as it is: towns with a Watch House against those without.** Every thief and killer begun in the campaigns above, counted each season it is at large: begun, not ended, and (a thief) not yet standing for judgement. One still at large when the count stops is counted as far as it got. "With" is a town where a Watch House stood for at least half of those seasons. The computer-played player answers none of them, so nobody is hunted on the leader's word: only the watch's own catches and the elders end them.
- **Raids held off with a town watch against without.** Every strike by a band on a held town. "With" is a town whose people had learned Town Watch and where a Watch House stood when the band struck.
- **People lost per band strike with a Fortress against without.** The killed and the carried off in each strike by a band on a held town, the strikes held off counted too (a Fortress holds more of them off). A town that fell to the band is counted by what the strike itself cost it.
- **Seasons a thief or a killer is at large, with a Watch House in every town against as it is (read with CRIME=1).** The same campaigns are told again with a Watch House in every town of the player's, which makes the report take about four times as long; run with CRIME=1.
- **Of the player's convoys a band lay in wait for, stopped with an Inn on the road against without.** The player's convoys that finished their road with a band lying in wait on it (highwaymen at either end of a stretch of it, or pirates where it crosses the sea), counted as each comes in. "With" is a convoy whose own road passes a town of the player's with an Inn standing. A convoy no band lay in wait for is never stopped, Inn or none, and is left out.
- **Stores a band takes from a convoy of the player's it stops, with Escorts against without.** Food, wood, gold and stone together, for each convoy stopped. "With" is a player who had learned Escorts by then.
- **Seasons running a hungry town stays hungry, with Paved Roads against without.** Every spell of hunger in a held town, every people's: the seasons from the first hungry one until it is fed, changes hands or the count stops. "With" is a town whose people had learned Paved Roads when the spell began. The spell ends when the town is fed, by a convoy or by anything else.
- **Convoys of the player's with an Inn on the road that a band in wait came on, against the same convoys with no Inn.** The Inn's rule (roads.ts): a band is half as likely to come on a convoy whose road passes a town of its people with an Inn. Every convoy of the player's with an Inn on its road and a band in wait, by the draw that decided it: those the band came on, against those it would have come on by the same draw with no Inn there. Fewer are robbed than are come on, Inn or none: an army of the player's on the road fights the band and may see it off.
- **Thieves taken in a season in a town with a Watch House, against the same seasons with none.** The Watch House's rule (watchhouse.ts): a thief is 15 in a hundred likelier to be taken each season. Every season a thief was at large in a town with a Watch House standing, any people's, by the draw that decided it.
- **Clues to a killer found in a season in a town with a Watch House, against the same seasons with none.** The Watch House's rule: a clue to a killer is 20 in a hundred likelier to turn up each season. Every season a killer was at large in a town with a Watch House standing, by the draw that decided it. Judged only over 2,000 seasons or more (a plain run has a few hundred, and read 14 to 21 on those; with CRIME=1 it has thousands and reads 18 steadily: under 20 because where the court and an army are in the town a clue is all but sure already).
- **Killings in a season by a killer in a town with a Watch House, against the same seasons with none.** The Watch House's rule: a killer's chance of killing in a season is three quarters of what it was. The same seasons, by the draw that decided each.
- **Thieves and killers beginning among the player's people with Town Watch learnt, against the same seasons without.** Town Watch's rule (townwatch.ts): three in ten fewer thieves and killers begin. Every season the player's towns were looked at for a new one while the player had Town Watch, by the draws that decided it.
- **Strikes held off by a town that keeps a town watch, against the same strikes with its company left out.** Town Watch's rule: a town with a Watch House stands a company stronger against a band. Every strike on such a town, any people's, fought again with that company left out of its works. The rule gives a company, not a share of strikes held, so there is no band.
- **Households that move between a people's towns, per nation per 20 years.** Every family that set out by road from one town of its people to another in the first twenty years (migration.ts migrateOf, the household of householdmove.ts: the couple, their children and an old one), every people's, over three peoples a campaign; the people a move counts those on the convoy, children included. Why: the push that sent them. Settlers who went to found a town on empty land are counted apart.
- **Soldiers named on the map for the player's nation, by a battle fought out there or an army's second win.** Every army of the player's that comes to have a soldier known by name while the campaign is undecided. "By a battle" is one fought out on the map that the army won felling at least NAMED.least, with nobody known by name (veteranhome.ts); "by a second win" is rivalrule.ts's rule. A battle fought in person names its own and is not counted.
- **Of the felled in a map battle that names a soldier, the share he is credited with.** veteranhome.ts: credited NAMED.share (0.35) of the felled, three at least (the least that makes a name, champion.ts), in a battle that felled NAMED.least (10) or more.
- **The markets' caravans: how many, how many come home, how many are robbed, and the gold they bring.** A town with a Market standing trades by road with the nearest other market town within CARAVANS.reach lands, its own people's or one at peace with it, and its caravan comes home the season after with gold; highwaymen and pirates on its road meet it as a convoy, and a band standing on its road may fall on it (caravans.ts).
- **The player reaches its first age.** The computer-played player puts by 50.0% of the supplies it has in hand each season until the price of its first age is put by, then takes it; later ages it takes by the rivals' rule (SAVE=0 turns that off).
- **Unrest begun in a town, and what weighed on it.** Every time unrest began in a held town (unrest.ts), with the things that made it likelier as they stood at the season's end; a town may have several. Unrest comes to a rising two seasons on unless an army, the leader or a steadfast people keeps order.
- **Towns that rise from unrest become free towns, and what becomes of them.** A town that throws off its people's rule is nobody's, with its people in it, and remembers whose it was (freetown.ts). The towns a conqueror's yoke loses are another matter (conquered.ts) and are not counted here.
- **A raid fought out a round at a time, against the old sum's lines.** Every strike on a town (not on a road). It is decided by mapbattle.ts's clash (raidclash.ts: a band's force worth 0.82 of itself; taken where breaking the town cost the band 16.0% of its weight or less and the town is not already bled). The old lines: held at 0.82 of the band's force, a taking under 0.4.
- **Bands that come from the far wilds.** Camps of raiders and slavers born in wild country that is still empty, the rim of wilderness at the map's edge above all (rim.ts, wilds.ts), that march in for a mark (bandwilds.ts), a campaign; and against every band of any kind that came in the campaigns above. There must be such a land free, with a way in of more than one land.
- **The march in from the far wilds: drawn by the richest mark, and as the nearest would have had it.** Every camp that set out from the far wilds, by the march it made as a host out of the wilds makes it (the softest and richest town within eight lands with no reckoning of the march, and of those nearly as good the farthest half the time: bandwilds.ts deepWay; a rim land with a way of three lands or more is taken first when one is born) and by the one the old weighing would have made (the softest mark weighed against the seasons of the march: bandroam.ts wayTo), which decides nothing now. Lands: from where it was born to where it makes its place; the mark is the town it means to strike from there, at that place or one land on; defended with: what that town has against a band (threats.ts defenceOf).
- **A band from the far wilds is seen before it strikes or arrives.** Seen: an army in or beside a land it passed, or a town within two lands (of the rim where it gathered, too), told its people. Ahead: from when it was first seen to when it struck or arrived, the mean over those seen that did either. Both must hold: eight in ten seen, and a season or more. Met: an army went against it (not the band against the army) before it had struck anyone.
- **Risings for a claimant to the throne against the player's nation, proclaimed in a child's name or by one come of age.** A plot by a lord who is not of the leader's faith in which he takes the leader's wife into his house; a child born there, or to a wife held by a band; a rising proclaimed in the child's name by whoever holds it, from half a year after the birth; and the child grown (HEIR_AGE), leading that rising or raising one. In band: 2 to 5 risings against the player every 100 campaigns, and at least one of them for a child born in a lord's house. Towns that rise for a claimant are counted here and not among the free towns risen from unrest.
- **Raids on a town with an army of its people in it, against without.** Every strike on a held town, not on a road: whether an army of the town's people stood in it when the band struck (threats.ts defenceOf counts it), and how those strikes went against the rest. Roads have their own guard and are left out, so the shares here are not the row below's.
- **A raid with the town's men turned out as bands, against the fight as it was.** Every strike on a town (not on a road), fought twice with the same band and the same day: as threats.ts decides it now, with the town's own men turned out as militia bands on foot beside any army and only its works, walls and guard as the place, as against a nation (militia.ts; stage three); and as it was decided before (the town a sum of its men, works, walls and army, its men part of the place). A town bled already cannot be taken either way. The old fight decides nothing: it is recorded on the raid for this row only, for a release.
- **Towns taken, by where the line for a taking lies.** A town is taken, not only raided, where breaking it cost the band no more than a share of its own weight (raidclash.ts RAID.easilyOut). Every strike where the town broke and had nobody in a band's hands already, by what that cost the band: so what another line would give can be read off. (These are the strikes of a world run at the line as it is; moved, the world answers back.)
- **Men a town loses to a strike, and what a battle with a nation would have cost it.** The men of a town killed in a strike are those its bands lost in the fight (militiatoll.ts: the men who stood, by the share of their strength lost in the fight and the rout), at a raid's own share dead (raidclash.ts RAID.dead): beside it, what the same losses would be at the share a battle with a nation goes by (FELL.dead, a half). By rule, before stage three, it was 0.6 a strike.
- **What a band takes from a town it breaks.** Every strike on a town (not on a road) that broke it: the goods of all four kinds taken from its stores, and for a taking the women (and, by slavers, men) carried off. By the rounds the fight ran before the town broke, since what a band takes follows how long it had the town and what it had left (raidclash.ts).
- **A band meets soldiers on the road: by the rule as it is, and fought out.** Each meeting of a band with soldiers away from a town's walls is decided by the fight (roadclash.ts); the rule it went by before is tallied beside it. hunt: an army of a computer people, or the report's player unwatched, goes against a band; ambush: a band falls on an army; convoy and caravan: highwaymen or pirates stop one that has an army of its people on its road; camp on a caravan: raiders, slavers or marauders fall on one so escorted; road: highwaymen strike a road's end where an army stands. By rule the stronger on the day wins (an escort as strong as the band sees it off); fought out, either may break or neither.
- **What a fight of an army and a band costs each side: fought out, and by the old shares.** Every fight of an army and a band with nobody watching (bandfight.ts fightByMeans): hunt, an army went against a band; ambush, a band fell on an army; won or lost by the army. Fought out (roadclash.ts) each loses what the fight took; by the old shares an army lost a quarter of its companies beating its equal and four tenths routed, and a band that beat an army off lost three tenths of the army's strength. A band the army beats is broken either way.
- **Captives got back when the player has an army near.** Every people's captives, the player's included: those whose people had an army at the band's place (either end of a highwaymen's road) or next door while they were held. The computer-played player sends an army it can reach and that is as strong as the band (attackBand, settled unwatched); rivals hunt as they do. Gone before the four seasons were up is got back. About half is taken as 35 to 65%.
- **Taken towns that rise if left ungarrisoned.** A town taken by force (it bears a yoke, stamped with the season of the capture), followed from then for twenty seasons, once for each capture; garrisoned if an army of its taker stood in it for over half of the seasons it was followed. One taken again by force before it rose or settled, or still followed when the run ended, is not counted either way.
- **A nation's men under arms.** Men with its armies (armytown.ts) over those and the men in its towns. In war is a season within a year of the nation winning or losing land; a free town that comes back by treaty (freetown.ts) is land gained with no war, and does not count.
- **A besieged town with walls or a fortress and its men (or an army) in it.** On the map: every siege of a nation's town settled in resolvePending, the rivals' among themselves included (campaign.ts battleWatchers), with a Fortress or a tower, wall or gate standing in its town, and its men or an army of its own in it. In real time: 8 sieges drawn like those a campaign has. A campaign is played on by the computer for 20 to 48 seasons, so that each people is at its own age, and then the rival comes against the player's capital (given a Fortress: it stands as towers in its town) with infantry and archers grown until the map rates the assault at 1 to 1.6 times the town's defence (its own measure, as in resolvePending); the computer plays both sides for up to 20 minutes; a town still standing then is counted as having held. Holds means the attacker was thrown back.
