Town and economy 5

Gathering

Working

Villagers bring in food, wood, gold and stone.

  • Berries, farms, hunting and fishing for food
  • Trees, gold and stone to work
  • Storehouses as drop-off points

Gaps

  • Every rate and cost is a guess: nobody has played it for balance

Building and walls

Working

Raise a town and fortify it.

  • 19 buildings across two build pages
  • Palisades, stone walls, gates and towers; drag to draw walls
  • A Wonder that wins the game if it stands

Gaps

  • Buildings need nearly level ground, but nothing tells you why a spot is refused

Ages and research

Working

Advance through five ages and buy upgrades.

  • 5 ages from Stone to Gunpowder
  • 21 upgrades: tools, weapons, armour, unit lines

Gaps

  • The research list is short and the same for every nation

Roads

Working, rough

Every building is joined to its town centre.

  • A road is laid the moment a building is placed, and joins nearby roads into streets
  • Units move faster on roads and their routes prefer them
  • Dirt tracks become cobbles from the Iron Age
  • Saved with the game

Gaps

  • You cannot lay or remove a road yourself
  • Roads do not join one town to another
  • Only checked on level ground

Market

Working, rough

Buy and sell resources for gold.

  • Buying and selling at the Market

Gaps

  • No trade routes, caravans or trade between players

People and family 2

Families

Working

Men and women marry into houses and raise children.

  • A man and a woman in a house raise villagers for half the food
  • Children must grow up before they work
  • Family names, renown for large families, four villager traits
  • Villagers grow old and die

Gaps

  • Nothing happens to a widow, a widower or an orphan
  • Children cannot be seized by Slavers

Morale and heroes

Working, rough

Homes steady the defenders; a hero leads the army.

  • Houses near the Town Center harden its defenders
  • Warlord and Strategist heroes with an aura

Gaps

  • Your nation's leader never appears on the battlefield: heroes are generic

Faith and vice 6

Piety and decadence

Working, rough

A town drifts between Righteous and Decadent, with boons and banes each way.

  • Five standings, each changing births, plague, gold, work and more
  • A town looks its standing: church bright or in ruins, tavern plain or hung with lanterns, people and houses drawn modest or gaudy
  • 9 dilemmas that ask you to choose
  • Each nation starts with its own leaning

Gaps

  • Only 9 dilemmas, so they repeat
  • The numbers behind every boon and bane are guesses
  • Which way each nation leans is my guess, not yours

Church

Working, rough

Villagers serve as monks and nuns; the town grows pious.

  • Up to four clergy per Church
  • More children, shorter plagues, some plagues prevented
  • A holy town trains Priests and refuses pagan units

Gaps

  • Monks and nuns do the same thing: no difference between men and women here, unlike the Tavern
  • No feast days
  • A Church near a Tavern has no effect on it

Tavern

Working, rough

Milkmaids bring gold, patrons drink; the town grows decadent.

  • Women become milkmaids (gold), men become patrons (faster soldiers)
  • Mercenaries for hire, who leave if unpaid
  • Slower work, fewer children, plague in a worldly town

Gaps

  • No rumours about rival towns
  • No brawls or debts

Slavers and work camps

Working, rough

A decadent town steals villagers from others.

  • Slavers seize enemy villagers and march them to a Work Camp
  • Captive men raise the whole town's output
  • Captive women add villagers

Gaps

  • The computer builds a camp and sends a pair of Slavers once Decadent, but they walk alone into the rival's town and mostly die; it never guards against them
  • A Normal or Hard computer now empties its tavern before it reaches Decadent, so it rarely gets there; an Easy one does not staff a tavern at all
  • How a human Decadent town actually fares (whether raiding keeps pace with its dying households) has not been played or measured
  • Night matters only to the work camp (escapes and risings); nothing else in the match changes with it
  • The computer posts no guards at its camp, so it loses captives every night
  • Escapes and risings are told in messages and by people appearing at the gate; no one is drawn climbing out or fighting
  • The captives' daily round has not been watched in play, only stills taken before they had moved
  • Captives cannot be rescued by the town that lost them

Realistic people (trial)

First pass

People drawn from a real human figure with a skeleton and recorded motion. Village men and women and four foot soldiers use it so far.

  • A public-domain human figure (Quaternius, CC0) with 53 bones and 16 recorded motions
  • Men and women are built differently on the skeleton itself: a woman has narrower shoulders and ribcage, slimmer arms and neck, wider hips, smaller hands and feet and a tapered jaw; a man is broader with a thicker neck and squarer jaw
  • Women wear a long dress with a linen apron, hair long, in a bun or under a coif; men a short tunic over breeches
  • Builds differ from person to person: slight, average or heavy, and a smaller or fuller chest
  • Skirts and tunic skirts are real cloth shapes that hang from the hips and swing with the legs
  • A face fitted to the surface of the head: eyes with whites and coloured irises, lids, brows, a nose, lips and ears; hair, and beards on some men
  • A fitted upper garment, finely made so its curves are smooth: it hides the base figure's seams and gives a woman's bust a rounded, natural shape under cloth
  • More wardrobe pieces for later use: straw hat, hood and cape, cap, turban, fur hat, circlet, cloak, fur collar, aprons, coloured trims, long robes for men
  • A wardrobe view for looking at outfits, with a lower camera
  • Armour as separate pieces: leather, bronze, iron and kettle helmets, a breastplate and pauldrons, painted ring mail, round shields
  • Weapons and tools in the hand: club, spear, sword, pike, axe, pick, hammer, hoe
  • Drawn this way: villagers, Clubman, Spearman, Swordsman, Pikeman
  • A woman's neckline, sleeves and head covering follow how pious or decadent her town is
  • Idle villagers sometimes stand talking; soldiers play a full sword stroke for each blow
  • `?people=0` in the address switches back to the old figures for comparison

Gaps

  • Every other unit, children and captives still use the old block figures, and the two styles clash side by side
  • Seen only in still frames: skirts in motion, blows, deaths and tools in use are unchecked
  • No bow, sling or riding motions, so archers and cavalry cannot move over yet
  • Nations do not yet dress differently on the new figure in play: the extra outfits exist but no unit wears them yet
  • No wounds or torn clothes on the new figure
  • The edge of an open neckline is slightly ragged, and the base figure's elbow joints still show faintly on bare arms
  • Expressions are fixed
  • Cost with a hundred people on screen unmeasured
  • Feet slide a little: the jog is not matched to ground speed

Land and wildlife

Working, rough

The ground of each country, what grows on it, and the animals that live there. Drawn only: none of it changes the rules.

  • Ground painted per country: green only near water in the desert, frosted grass and a shingle shore in the north, long dunes, snow drifts, flower meadows, dark leaf litter under trees, marsh pools
  • Five kinds of tree built from trunk, boughs and leaf clusters: oak, birch, fir with tiered boughs (snow-laden in the north), palm and acacia; two shapes of each, and the odd one turned gold
  • Which trees grow depends on the country: palms and acacias in the desert, fir and birch in the north
  • Bushes and berry bushes are thickets of leaf clusters; grass tufts are fuller
  • Bushes, boulders, reeds, cacti, fallen logs, mushrooms and lily pads, cleared away where you build or a road is laid
  • Grass and reeds sway in the wind
  • Flocks of birds with shadows, gulls over water, vultures over desert and steppe
  • Butterflies by day and fireflies by night in green country
  • Rabbits (white hares in the north) that bolt when a unit comes near
  • Fish leaping in open water, tumbleweed in dry country, pollen and dust in the air
  • Smoke from the Town Center and lived-in houses, thicker at night and in the cold

Gaps

  • Motion checked in still frames only
  • Trees do not sway
  • Leaves are faceted masses, not individual leaves or textures
  • Wildlife cost unmeasured on a phone
  • Fireflies are faint
  • No sounds for any of it

Play styles

First pass

Every nation belongs to a group that says how it plays. Only the raiders change the rules so far.

  • Seven groups: raiders (Norse, Mongols), builders (Romans, Babylonians, Egyptians), farmers (Franks, English), scholars (Chinese, Greeks), the faithful (Israelites), traders (Persians, Arabs), warriors (Japanese)
  • Raiders start as a war band: six fighters, a man and a woman, and no Town Center
  • They may raise a Town Center in the Stone Age, wherever they choose to settle
  • Any raider fighter on foot or horse carries off an enemy villager, who joins the tribe as a villager; men and women alike
  • Idle raiders take villagers alive on sight instead of killing them
  • A captive is freed if the raider leading them home is killed
  • A band with no buildings is beaten only when its last person falls
  • Raiders train their own villagers half again as slowly, so they must take people to grow
  • The computer settles its band and sends one to three fighters out to take people

Gaps

  • The other six groups are labels only: they have no rules of their own yet
  • A raiding computer opponent lost 3 of 4 test games to a settled one
  • No drop-off point until the band settles
  • Raiders cannot be told to kill a villager while their hands are free
  • Not played by hand
  • No campaign-map version of raiding

Look and sound 6

3D world

Working, rough

The battlefield and its light.

  • Painted ground, scattered grass and stones
  • Shores run in smooth curves, not tile steps: the land falls away into the bed along a rounded line and the water thins to a soft edge of surf just short of it
  • Water takes its colour from the country: bright and clear in the desert, green over a temperate shelf, grey and cold in the north; rain rings its surface and the moon glints on it at night
  • Water with a moving surface of swell and chop: it mirrors the sky at a low angle and shows its depth looking down, catches the sun in points of glitter, shelves over several tiles from clear turquoise shallows with a net of light on the bed to deep blue, sends line after line of surf in to the beach, washes up and back along the sand, greys under weather and darkens at night
  • Hills that rise and fall and mountain ranges with snow on the peaks
  • Low warm sunlight, soft shadows, haze and drifting cloud shadows
  • A drawn finish: banded shade with an ink outline, heavy on buildings and fine on people
  • Weather that comes and goes: rain, snowfall in the north, dust in the desert

Gaps

  • Weather tells on fighting and movement (rain shortens bows and puts out fires, snow slows travel off the roads, dust cuts sight and bows) but not on harvests
  • The computer opponent does not plan around the weather
  • No seasons, no thunder, no sound of rain
  • Water mirrors a sky with drifting cloud but not the land or ships; ships ride the long swell but not the small chop
  • The water costs more to draw than anything else on the ground and has not been measured on a phone
  • Rain rings on water and ships riding the swell run but have not been seen
  • How fast it runs on a phone or in a big battle has not been measured

Buildings

Working, rough

What a town is made of.

  • Every building shaped by hand, with a ruined church for a decadent town: pitched roofs with eaves, timber framing, tiles and thatch, stonework, round towers, props
  • A building going up rises from its footings inside a scaffold of poles; roofs differ a shade from house to house
  • Walls join up: each piece reaches toward its neighbours, corners and ends get a heavier post, gates turn to stand across the wall
  • Each nation keeps its own roofs (domes, pagodas, flat roofs, tents) and colours by age
  • Church and tavern drawn bright, plain, ruined or gaudy by the town's standing
  • Each nation's people look like where they come from: fair in the north and west of Europe, olive round the Mediterranean, tan to brown in the Near East and on the Nile, light tan with black hair on the steppe and in the Far East; within a people the shades are close, and a captive keeps the look of the people they were taken from
  • People differ from one another: hair worn short, long, knotted or balding, beards and moustaches on some men, and faces with brows, a nose, a mouth and ears
  • What flies has its own shape and ending: fletched arrows and bolts point first, stones and boulders tumbling, cannon balls nearly flat; they land in dust, a grey puff or a splash, and a boulder shakes the ground
  • An engine or ship below half strength burns and smokes, worse as she nears her end; a troop ship shows up to six of those aboard
  • A ship that is sunk breaks up: masts, yards and sails come down around her, her crew go into the water, and the hull rolls over and settles by the stern in foam; a wrecked engine throws its crew clear
  • Siege engines are built as machines with their working parts and a crew: a catapult with its skein, stop bar and winch; a ram hung on chains under a roof of hides; a bolt-thrower with spring frame and slide; a trebuchet with trestles, counterweight box and sling; a banded great gun on a field carriage; a two-tier organ gun behind a mantlet
  • Four ships: a fishing boat with a lateen sail, net and catch; a galley with a ram, shields along the rail and five oars a side; a troop cog with castles and cargo; a warship with bowsprit, two masts with topsails and guns run out
  • Clothes have collars, cuffs, stitched hems, belt buckles and lacing; boots have soles and turned cuffs; hands have thumbs
  • Village women dress as their town lives: scarves where it is pious, laced bodices worn low where it is decadent, lower still at the extreme
  • In a worldly or decadent town a tavern with both maids and patrons almost never has a child (tens of minutes apart at most), and then one no household owns to; hearts rise from its busy tavern and from houses where a couple is at home, and a maid and a patron are found in each other's arms round the side
  • A day is five minutes, its last three tenths night: the light goes blue and dim and the top bar says so
  • The work camp is a four-tile compound of two rooms: a workroom where the women are kept at the querns by day, and a bunkhouse the men go out from to break stone and back into; at dusk all are shut into the bunkhouse and a lamp shows
  • Slavers can be posted at a camp as guards (up to four); each makes its men count a quarter more toward the town's work
  • Holding people has a cost: each guard watches three captives; by night an unwatched one slips away about once a night and runs for home, and a camp with four or more men unwatched may rise, burn and lose half of all it holds at a stroke
  • A Decadent town is dying out and grows by what it takes: its households have children less than half as often and lose half of them, villagers take twice as long to raise at the Town Center, and its Work Camp holds twelve
  • A villager taken by a Slaver is seen: stripped to a loincloth (a woman keeps a band across the chest), barefoot, roped at the neck and wrists, led behind the Slaver and then held in the work camp yard, the men at labour
  • A decadent town has women's clothes left about its tavern and two houses in three: a dress in a heap, a shift, shoes, and one hung by the door; a pious town keeps flowers at its doors

Gaps

  • Walls have not been checked on sloping ground or beside towers
  • One wonder for every nation, differing only in its roof
  • Houses come in four shapes with varied yards (woodpile, barrel, vegetable patch, washing line, well); the storehouse and barracks have two; the steppe keeps its houses, town centre, church, tavern, storehouse, barracks, smithy, stable, range and workshop under round felt tents (its market, tower and dock are still walled); every other building still has one shape
  • Nations share one set of building shapes under their own roofs

People

Working, rough

How men, women and soldiers look and move.

  • Men broad in the shoulder, women with a waist, bust and full skirt; faces, hands, boots, three hairstyles
  • Villagers dress for their work: woodcutter, farmer, hunter, forager, miner, builder
  • Milkmaids, patrons, monks and nuns stand at their building, dressed to the town's standing
  • Recorded motions on foot, with elbows and knees that bend: walk, run, work, strike, stab, shoot, cast, two deaths
  • Loose things bounce and swing: chests, hair, skirts, hats, shields, weapons, packs

Gaps

  • Motion has only been checked in still frames, never watched
  • Four-legged motion is a made-up trot (jointed legs, nodding head, flicking tail, rider rising to the stride, horses rearing to strike), not recorded motion, and has only been seen in stills
  • Wheels turn, galleys row, pennants fly and guns kick back, but none of it has been watched moving
  • Swords, helmets, belts and shields are rounded, but spears, guns, axes and the rarer units' gear are not
  • Engine and ship crews are half-size; thrown clear when their machine is destroyed, they tumble and lie like wreckage rather than dying as people do
  • Gun flash and smoke, wakes, projectiles in flight, their impacts and burning engines have run without errors but have not been seen: the one staged barrage was out of frame
  • Tavern and church staff move (patrons cheer, the maid serves, monks and nuns bow) but this has not been watched
  • No generated models installed

Damage and death

Working, rough

What fighting does to people and buildings.

  • Soldiers lose shields, helmets and armour as they weaken
  • Villagers' clothes tear to show skin, and they die in rags
  • People fall with a recorded death and drop their weapons; beasts topple, engines fly apart, ships sink
  • Blood and wounds can be switched off

Gaps

  • Torn clothes are flat patches, not real rips
  • Riders do not fall from their horses
  • Whether the dead end up lying flat has not been confirmed
  • The switch for blood is an address option, not a setting in the menu
